# Emergency Shock Management & Positioning

> **EMERGENCY NOTICE:** Physical shock occurs when internal blood flow and oxygen delivery to vital organs drop critically. Shock is a life-threatening condition that can follow severe trauma, allergic reactions, burns, or heavy blood loss. Call emergency services (112 / 911 / 108) immediately.

---

### Recognizing Warning Signs of Shock
- Skin that is pale, cool, ashen, or clammy to the touch.
- Rapid, shallow, or irregular breathing.
- Weak, rapid pulse (thread-like heart rate).
- Intense thirst, dry mouth, or nausea.
- Dizziness, lightheadedness, weakness, anxiety, or gradual drowsiness and fainting.

---

### Step-by-Step Response Protocol

#### 1. Eliminate the Trigger Cause (If Possible)
- If visible severe bleeding is present, immediately apply firm direct pressure as detailed in the Severe Bleeding guide.
- If an allergic reaction (anaphylaxis) is suspected and the person has an emergency auto-injector, help them administer it.

#### 2. Emergency Shock Positioning (Trendelenburg Variant)
- Lay the person flat on their back on a blanket or comfortable surface.
- **Elevate both legs:** Raise their feet and lower legs 20 to 30 cm (8 to 12 inches) above heart level, resting on a bag, folded blanket, or chair.
- *Elevation promotes blood return to critical organs (brain and heart).*
- **Positioning Exceptions:**
  - If a head, neck, spine, or leg fracture is suspected: **Keep the person completely flat** without moving legs.
  - If the person has difficulty breathing or chest pain: Allow them to rest in a comfortable semi-seated position.
  - If vomiting or bleeding from the mouth: Turn them onto their side (Recovery Position) to avoid airway blockage.

#### 3. Maintain Normal Body Warmth
- Cover the person with a warm blanket, coat, or emergency foil thermal sheet.
- Protect them from cold ground underneath by sliding cardboard or a second blanket under their body.
- Prevent shivering, but do not apply intense artificial heating pads.

#### 4. Loosen Restrictive Clothing
- Unbutton tight collars, belts, waistbands, or neckties to reduce physical restrictions on breathing and blood flow.

#### 5. Monitor Vital Signs Continuously
- Stay with the person and talk to them to keep them calm.
- Check breathing regularity every 2 minutes. If breathing stops, immediately start Hands-Only CPR.

---

### Critical Precautions
- ❌ **Do not give food or drink:** Even if the person complains of intense thirst, do not give water or liquids. Anesthesia may be required upon hospital arrival, and liquids risk severe vomiting and aspiration.
- ❌ **Do not move the person unnecessarily.** Unneeded movement accelerates shock.
