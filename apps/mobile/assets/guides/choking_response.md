# Choking Relief & Airway Clearance

> **EMERGENCY NOTICE:** If the person cannot breathe, speak, cough, or turns blue/gray, have someone call emergency services (112 / 911 / 108) immediately while you start assistance.

---

### Recognizing Severe Airway Obstruction
- **Universal Choking Sign:** Person clutches both hands to their throat.
- **Inability to Speak or Cry:** Cannot produce sound, voice is silent or high-pitched wheeze.
- **Weak or Ineffective Cough:** Person cannot cough forcefully enough to clear the obstruction.
- **Skin Color Changes:** Lips, nail beds, or face turn blue, purplish, or dusky gray.

*Note:* If the person is coughing forcefully and can speak, encourage them to keep coughing. Do not perform physical thrusts if they are moving air effectively.

---

### Step-by-Step Response (Adults & Children over 1 year)

#### The "5-and-5" Protocol

#### 1. Give 5 Firm Back Blows
- Stand to the side and slightly behind the choking person.
- Place one arm across the person's chest for upper body support.
- Bend the person forward at the waist so their upper airway faces downward (helping gravity dislodge the object).
- Deliver **5 separate, firm blows** between the shoulder blades using the heel of your free hand.
- Check if each blow has successfully expelled the object before delivering the next.

#### 2. Give 5 Abdominal Thrusts (Heimlich Maneuver)
If the object is not expelled after 5 back blows:
- Stand directly behind the person and wrap both arms around their waist.
- Make a fist with one hand and place the thumb side just above the person's navel, well below the breastbone.
- Grasp your fist with your other hand.
- Pull inward and upward forcefully into the abdomen in a quick, distinct thrust.
- Deliver **up to 5 abdominal thrusts**.

#### 3. Alternate Until Cleared
- Continue alternating between **5 back blows** and **5 abdominal thrusts** until the obstruction is dislodged or the person becomes unresponsive.

---

### If the Person Becomes Unresponsive
1. Lower them carefully to the floor onto their back.
2. Ensure emergency services (112 / 911) are contacted immediately.
3. Begin chest compressions immediately (Hands-Only CPR protocol: 30 compressions).
4. Each time before delivering air or continuing, open the mouth wide: **look for the foreign object**. If clearly visible, gently sweep it out with a finger.
5. **Never perform a blind finger sweep**, as this can push an unseen object deeper into the airway.

---

### Self-Response (If You Are Choking Alone)
1. Dial local emergency services and leave the line open.
2. Place a fist slightly above your navel, grasp with the other hand, and pull inward and upward.
3. Alternatively, lean over the back of a firm chair, table edge, or railing and thrust your upper abdomen downward against the edge.
