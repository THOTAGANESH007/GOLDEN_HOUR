# Fracture & Bone Injury Immobilization

> **EMERGENCY NOTICE:** For open fractures (bone protruding through skin), suspected spinal/neck injuries, or intense trauma, do not move the person. Call local emergency services (112 / 911 / 108) immediately.

---

### Signs of a Broken Bone or Severe Joint Injury
- Severe localized pain aggravated by any movement or pressure.
- Visible deformity, unnatural angle, or shortening of the limb.
- Rapid swelling, bruising, or inability to bear weight.
- Grinding sensation (*crepitus*) or sound during movement.

---

### Step-by-Step Response Protocol

#### 1. Keep the Person Calm & Still
- Encourage the injured person to remain still and avoid moving the affected limb.
- Do not attempt to force, realign, or push back a deformed bone or dislocated joint.

#### 2. Control Any External Bleeding
- If an open wound accompanies the injury, cover the wound with a clean sterile dressing.
- Apply gentle pressure around the wound edges to control bleeding without pushing on bone fragments.

#### 3. Immobilize the Injured Limb
The golden rule of immobilization is to support the joint **above** and the joint **below** the injury site.
- **Use Rigid Supports:** Use rolled magazines, sturdy cardboard, wooden slats, or folded umbrellas as improvised splints.
- **Pad the Splint:** Wrap the rigid splint with soft cloths, towels, or cotton to prevent pressure sores against bony areas.
- **Secure the Splint:** Fasten the splint on both sides using cloth ties, bandaging, or tape. Secure firmly above and below the fracture, but never directly over the break.
- **Check Circulation:** Check that fingers or toes beyond the splint remain warm and pink. If skin turns cold, pale, or numb, loosen ties immediately.

#### 4. Support with an Arm Sling (Upper Limb)
- For collarbone, shoulder, or forearm injuries, support the arm using a triangular cloth sling or a folded jacket pinned across the chest to prevent movement.

#### 5. Apply Cold Pack for Swelling
- Place ice wrapped in a damp cloth or towel against the injured area for up to 15-20 minutes at a time.
- **Never place bare ice directly onto the skin.**

---

### Suspected Neck, Spine, or Pelvis Injuries
- If an individual was involved in a fall from height, road collision, or diving incident:
  - **Do not move or turn the person** unless they face an immediate life hazard (fire, rising water).
  - Kneel at their head, place both hands firmly on either side of their head, and hold the head and neck in steady alignment with the torso.
  - Wait for trained emergency rescue personnel to arrive with rigid backboards.
