# Seizure Bystander Safety Protocol

> **EMERGENCY NOTICE:** Call emergency services (112 / 911 / 108) if the seizure lasts longer than 5 minutes, if a second seizure begins immediately, if the person has difficulty breathing after it ends, or if the person is injured or pregnant.

---

### What Occurs During an Active Seizure
A generalized seizure involves sudden, involuntary muscle contractions, stiffness, rhythmic jerking of limbs, temporary loss of awareness, and possible shallow breathing or lip cyanosis. Most seizures resolve naturally within 1 to 3 minutes.

---

### Step-by-Step Bystander Response

#### 1. Protect the Person from Surrounding Hazards
- Clear away hard, sharp, or hot objects (furniture, glass, tools) within reaching distance.
- Guide the person away from stairs, roadways, or edge hazards if it starts while they are standing.
- Gently guide them to the floor if possible.

#### 2. Protect Their Head
- Place a soft cushion, folded jacket, or pillow gently under their head to prevent head impact trauma against the hard floor.
- Loosen tight neckwear, ties, or collar buttons that may restrict breathing.
- Remove eyeglasses to prevent breakage.

#### 3. Note the Start Time
- Look at your watch or phone clock.
- **Timing is critical:** If the active convulsive phase exceeds 5 minutes, it is a critical emergency requiring rapid intervention.

#### 4. Place in Recovery Position After Jerking Stops
Once active convulsing subsides:
- Gently roll the person onto their side into the **Recovery Position**.
- Tilt their head slightly backward with the chin pointing upward to keep the airway open.
- This position allows saliva or vomit to drain freely from the mouth rather than obstructing the lungs.

#### 5. Stay Calm & Comfort Them
- Speak quietly and reassuringly as they regain consciousness.
- Many individuals experience a post-seizure confused period (*post-ictal state*) lasting 10 to 30 minutes. Stay with them until they are fully alert and oriented.

---

### Critical Warnings: What NEVER to Do
- ❌ **NEVER put anything in the person's mouth!** A person cannot swallow their tongue. Inserting spoons, fingers, or cloth can chip teeth, bite your fingers, or block their airway.
- ❌ **NEVER hold the person down or physically restrain them.** Fighting muscle contractions can cause bone fractures and severe muscle tears.
- ❌ **NEVER give food, water, or medication** until they are completely awake, alert, and capable of swallowing safely.
