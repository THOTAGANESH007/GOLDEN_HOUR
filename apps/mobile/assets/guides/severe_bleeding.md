# Severe Bleeding & Hemorrhage Response

> **EMERGENCY NOTICE:** Rapid blood loss is a critical emergency. Call local emergency services (112 / 911 / 108) immediately and apply continuous pressure without delay.

---

### Immediate Action Priorities
Your primary objective is to stop or slow blood loss until emergency responders arrive on scene.

---

### Step-by-Step Response Protocol

#### 1. Ensure Scene Safety & Protection
- Confirm the surrounding environment is safe from hazards (traffic, machinery, electrical wires).
- If available, wear protective nitrile gloves or use a clean barrier to avoid direct blood contact.

#### 2. Expose the Wound
- Remove or cut clothing over the injured area to clearly identify the exact origin of bleeding.
- Look for spurting, pulsing, or continuous heavy pooling blood.

#### 3. Apply Firm Direct Pressure
- Place a clean cloth, sterile gauze pad, or clothing directly over the bleeding site.
- If no cloth is available, use your bare hands with firm pressure.
- Press down firmly with both hands, locking your arms and using your body weight to maintain steady force.
- **Do not let up on pressure:** Maintain continuous, hard pressure for at least 5 to 10 minutes without lifting the cloth to check.

#### 4. Reinforce — Do Not Remove Soaked Dressings
- If blood soaks through the dressing, do not remove the original layer (doing so dislodges blood clots).
- Add additional layers of cloth or pads directly on top and continue pressing firmly.

#### 5. Wound Packing (For Deep Wounds on Groin, Shoulder, Neck)
- If bleeding originates from a deep puncture or cavity in areas where a tourniquet cannot be placed:
  - Firmly pack sterile gauze or clean cloth directly into the wound until filled.
  - Apply intense two-handed pressure directly over the packed wound.

#### 6. Tourniquet Safety (For Limb Bleeding Only)
If severe, life-threatening spurting blood on an arm or leg does not stop with direct pressure:
- Apply a commercial tourniquet 5 to 7 cm (2 to 3 inches) above the bleeding site (between the wound and the heart).
- Do not apply a tourniquet directly over a joint (elbow or knee); place it above the joint.
- Tighten the windlass until the bleeding stops completely and pulse downstream is absent.
- Secure the windlass rod in its clip.
- Note the exact time of application on the tourniquet band or person's skin (e.g., *"TK 14:35"*).
- **Never loosen or remove a tourniquet** once applied; only emergency hospital responders should remove it.

---

### What NOT to Do
- ❌ **Do not remove an impaled object** (such as glass, knife, or rebar). Secure the object in place with bulky padding around it to prevent further tissue damage.
- ❌ **Do not apply a makeshift wire or narrow cord** as a tourniquet; thin materials can cut nerve tissue. Use broad fabric (at least 2 inches wide) if an emergency improvised tourniquet is necessary.
- ❌ **Do not allow the injured person to stand or walk.** Keep them lying down to preserve blood pressure and reduce shock.
