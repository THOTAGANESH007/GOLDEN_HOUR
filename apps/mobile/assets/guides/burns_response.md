# Burn Response & Heat Injury Safety

> **EMERGENCY NOTICE:** For large burns (larger than the person's palm), facial, hand, joint, or chemical/electrical burns, dial emergency services (112 / 911 / 108) immediately.

---

### Identifying Burn Severity
- **Superficial Burns:** Red, dry skin with pain (similar to mild sunburn). No blisters.
- **Partial-Thickness Burns:** Deep redness, swelling, intense pain, and fluid-filled blisters.
- **Full-Thickness Burns:** Leathery, charred black, or waxy white skin. May be painless due to nerve damage. Always requires urgent emergency response.

---

### Step-by-Step Response Protocol

#### 1. Stop the Burning Process
- Extinguish flames or remove the person from the heat source safely.
- If clothing is catching fire, use the *"Stop, Drop, and Roll"* technique or smother with a heavy blanket.
- For electrical burns: Ensure power is disconnected before touching the person.

#### 2. Cool with Clean Running Water Immediately
- Cool the burned area immediately under gentle, cool running tap water for **10 to 20 minutes**.
- **Important:** Use cool or tepid water, **never ice or freezing water**, as extreme cold damages delicate skin tissue.
- If running water is unavailable, apply clean, cool wet compresses, changing them frequently.

#### 3. Remove Constricting Items Gently
- Immediately remove jewelry, watches, rings, belts, and tight clothing near the burned area before swelling develops.
- **Caution:** If clothing is melted or stuck to the burn, **do not rip or pull it off**. Cut around the stuck fabric.

#### 4. Cover with a Sterile, Non-Adherent Dressing
- Cover the cooled burn loosely with clean plastic cling film (kitchen wrap) or a sterile, non-fluffy dressing.
- Cling film provides an ideal temporary protective barrier that does not stick to burned tissue.
- Do not wrap tightly; leave room for natural tissue swelling.

#### 5. Chemical Burns Protocol
- Protect yourself from splash contamination.
- Brush dry powdered chemicals off the skin using a dry cloth before rinsing.
- Flush skin or eyes thoroughly with continuous clean running water for at least **20 minutes**.
- Preserve the chemical container or label if safe, to assist emergency teams.

---

### Critical Warnings & What NOT to Do
- ❌ **Do not apply butter, oil, toothpaste, turmeric, or grease.** These hold heat inside the tissue and cause severe infection.
- ❌ **Do not pop or puncture blisters.** Blisters act as a natural protective sterile barrier against bacteria.
- ❌ **Do not use ice, ice packs, or freezing cold water.** Extreme cold causes hypothermia and further tissue necrosis.
- ❌ **Do not overcool large body areas.** Excessive cold application on large burns can cause dangerous body cooling (hypothermia).
